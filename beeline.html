Beeline, and the systems under it

Beeline is the young person’s version of the MBacc, Greater Manchester’s framework of technical and academic routes into work. It runs across the region’s ten local authorities and serves people up to 25 years old.

Design assurance

Two metrics, in action

LM-03 asks whether young people leave with more options than they arrived with. A session counts as broadening when it explores two or more . This is the first month it could be read: between two thirds and four fifths of sessions narrow to one gateway, and the ones that broaden cluster in Digital and Engineering. That reading is what the Discovery Quiz redesign is now aimed at, and it gave the board a baseline to judge the next release against.

Explored two or more gateways Narrowed to one Paired-gateway sessions, 6 July to 4 August 2026. Percentages rounded to the nearest five.

Reading it without fooling ourselves. Last year the seven gateways sat in a fixed order. Once the order was randomised, sessions comparing two or more gateways went from 20.9% to 17.2% (z = −6.75, p < 0.001). Part of last year’s figure was people clicking the next card down, so the lower number is the truer reading. Digital and Financial stay paired in either order; Creative and Digital broke the pattern, and that is the next thing to look at. I read each step with Alex Edmans’ Ladder of Misinference: is the number accurate, representative, conclusive, and does it hold everywhere?

LM-05 asks whether interest turns into a real next step, counted as outbound clicks to live job searches and technical courses. People who finish the Discovery Quiz open more than double the job searches of people who browse the landing page, and more than any of the seven gateway pages manages on its own. The best of those, Finance, Business & Professional Services, reaches 4.0.

Job searches opened per person Averages, rounded to one decimal place.

This is the clearest evidence the quiz does the job it was built for, so we adapted the user journey to include it. It is also why step-level tracking is the open ask: how many people start it, and how many finish, is still invisible.
