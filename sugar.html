THE BRIEF

What the institute asked for

WHAT THEY HADAn artificial photosynthesis system turning CO₂ into carbon monoxide, formic acid, methanol, ethanol and ethylene using solar energy. Built at lab scale, with no market.Technology Readiness Level 4: still a lab technology.
THE CHALLENGE, AS WRITTENHow might we develop an innovative service based on the “Artificial photosynthesis technology” and create a business opportunity out of it?
THE METHOD · THREE WORLDS

Three diamonds, diverge and converge each time

  1. ◇ NEEDFINDING · AUTUMNWhere could it go?Desk research across nine fields, case studies of comparable technologies, expert interviews, personas.
  2. ◇ PROTOTYPING · WINTERDoes it work there?Plastics taken forward. Critical experience prototypes, then dark horse and funky prototypes, then one merged functional system.
  3. ◆ ONBOARDING · SPRINGWhat is the real problem?The challenge turned around. Design vision, solution, brand and interface, built and tested against a real case.

NINE FIELDS, NARROWED TWICE

ChimneysCement productionFashionBuilding sectorTransportation★ Plastic upcyclingAgricultureDigital pollutionWaste sector

Pass one used three guiding features: desirability, technical feasibility, economic viability. Pass two used eight selection criteria, voted on directly on a mind map of every application we had found. Plastics upcycling was taken forward.

PLOT TWIST · THE U-TURN

The deadlock underneath

THE SCIENTISTSNeed companies to invest so the technology can be developed further.
⇄
THE COMPANIESNeed the technology developed further before they are comfortable investing.
THE CHALLENGE, REWRITTENHow might we create a reliable system around the artificial leaf technology during its development phase in order to onboard companies?

WHAT WE DELIVERED · CO/LAB

Software that simulates installing the leaf in a specific factory, so the institute and a company can work through the technical, physical and financial logistics together, on editable templates, in the same room.

  1. 01 · SURVEY ✓Emissions, emissions taxes, space and the output wanted.
  2. 02 · TECHNICAL SIMULATION ✓Location and output volume produce a detailed simulation.
  3. 03 · FINANCIAL ESTIMATION ✓A benefits evaluation and a costs-and-benefits analysis.
  4. 04 · 3D MODEL ✓The leaf shown on a company’s own site, built with the industry partner.

Service blueprint
THE BUILD-OUT · FOUR LEVELS

We set the tool out in four levels, each adding interaction and needing more investment. We worked through all four with IIT and secured an industry partner, who carried them out.

  1. LEVEL 1 ✓A spreadsheet of technical and financial calculations, a Figma view of the software, and a shared folder of simulations for IIT’s first company meetings.
  2. LEVEL 2 ✓A survey companies fill in before a meeting, so they arrive prepared.
  3. LEVEL 3 ✓A software engineer turns the spreadsheet into customisable software.
  4. LEVEL 4 ✓3D simulations of the leaf on a company’s own site, built with the companies.

LOOKING BACK

What I would do differently

Today I would map the landscape of companies first and work out a way in for IIT step by step. It takes longer. With the leaf at Readiness Level 4, I would also standardise how its impact is shown to each kind of stakeholder before discussing what it could look like on a company’s own site.
