The jobs behind it

Two people, one decision

Both jobs start at the same moment, and separate only in what each person wants from it.

When

a student finishes the core questions…

The teacher

I want to know whether to challenge or support them next,

so thatI don’t waste their time on questions they’re not ready for, or bore them with ones they’ve already shown they know.

The student

I want the next questions to match what I actually know,

so thatI don’t feel punished for struggling or bored by repetition.

The decision

Core questions, then two ways on

The fork sits at the end of the core set. What a student did there decides which path opens next, so the same lesson serves a student who is ready to stretch and one who needs another way in.

Stretch Support The route is chosen by the student’s own results, and the teacher sees which one opened.

What it serves

One fork, two jobs

For the teacher

The decision is made at the point the evidence exists, so a teacher reads a result and acts on it before the next lesson.

For the student

The next questions match what they have shown, so struggling opens a different route rather than the same one again.
