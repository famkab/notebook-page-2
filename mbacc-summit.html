MBacc Summit

The day the work went public. Beeline was the centrepiece of the 2026 summit, in front of schools, employers and organisations from across Greater Manchester, in the same month the MBacc became national policy.

My role Manchester · July 2026
Role Programme Manager, Youth Employment & Opportunity Digital Services
Led on I ran the floor on the day, and was on stage with young people walking the audience through what we had released.
Team The wider digital team, the wider YEO team, and young people from across the ten boroughs.
Why it mattered

The date everything pointed at

The summit was the fixed point the delivery plan was built backwards from. It decided what had to be finished, what could be learned afterwards, and what was not going to make it.

The room

Schools, colleges, employers and partner organisations from all ten boroughs in one place. For most of them it was the first time they had seen the service working.

What was different this year

Beeline was the centrepiece of the day, and it landed in the same month the became national policy.

On stage

Young people did the demonstrating

The release was walked through on stage by young people playing the Discovery Game. They had the cards, the tally and the microphone, and the room watched a real session. It is the Influence part of the in its most literal form: the people who shaped it are the ones who present it to the people who fund it.

On the floor

Running the floor

Away from the stage, the toolkit was in people’s hands all day: schools and organisations trying the cards, asking what it costs them to run, and telling us what would stop them using it. Those conversations went into the log the same way a session in a youth centre does.

What I took from it

Three things the day settled

A public date is a design constraint. Knowing exactly what the room would see in July made every scoping argument in the months before it concrete.

Watching a service used in front of an audience surfaces things no test session does. The parts people leaned in for, and the parts that needed explaining out loud, both went straight into the next round.

Putting young people on the stage rather than quoting them is the difference between consultation and influence. Partners in the room remembered that.
